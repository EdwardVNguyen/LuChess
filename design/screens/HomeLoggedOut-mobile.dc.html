How it works

Read it. Connect it. Play it.

1

Read

Start with articles and annotated games by people who care about chess, not an engine’s evaluation bar.

2

Connect

Turn each game into a note. Link it to the openings, ideas and endgames it reminds you of.

3

Play

Challenge a friend or a bot on the exact line you studied, and see whether it stuck.

Luhmann’s method

Ideas stick when they connect.

Niklas Luhmann filled thousands of linked notes and said they did his thinking with him. Chess Notes brings that habit to your games.

  • One note for each game, named however you like
  • Link games to each other the way Obsidian does
  • Short comments that capture one big idea, not a wall of moves
Learn from people

Good resources beat more engine lines.

The games you remember have a story. Chess Notes is built around articles and notes from real players, with their reasoning laid out where you can follow it.

  • Read articles in a clean, book-like Read view
  • Follow creators and topics you trust
  • Add your own notes to an article for everyone to learn from

Practice by playing

Then prove it over the board.

Pick the opening you just learned and challenge someone to play it with you. Your opponent gets a cheat sheet, so the line is always there to practice.

  • Your opponent follows the opening you chose
  • A few take-backs before any penalty, and you set the rule
  • Bots are always ready when no one else is
