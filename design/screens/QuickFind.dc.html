The big idea

Hanging pawns are strong only while they can advance. Trade off their defenders and freeze them, and they become the target.

Line of thought

1.2.3.4.5.6.7.

The Tartakower Variation: Black fianchettoes and invites trades to relieve the cramp. A surprise from a lifelong 1.e4 player, who opens 1.c4.

8.9.10.

The trades ease Black's position, but they leave a fixed pawn on d5, and that structure is what the rest of the game is about.

11.12.13.114.15.

Black gets hanging pawns on c5 and d5. They are strong while they can advance and weak the moment they are frozen.

16.17.18.19.20.121.

Fischer trades the knight for the bishop that guarded d5, then strikes in the centre. The pawns stop being a strength and become targets.

0 / 280
Sub-variation at move 7

7…Ne4, the Lasker Defence: the same trades by a different route.

On 13.Qa3

13.Qa3 hits c5 at once and ties Black’s rook to its defence. A small move that already shows Fischer’s plan.

On 20.e4

The whole question with hanging pawns is whether they can advance. Here 20.e4 answers it before Black is ready.
