Fischer v. Spassky 1972, Game 6

How a quiet queen’s gambit turned into a lesson about hanging pawns.

IC
Ines Calloway8 min read · Updated October 28
Follow214

Game 6 is often named the best game of the 1972 match. Fischer, a lifelong 1.e4 player, began with 1.c4, and the game soon settled into a Queen’s Gambit Declined with Black choosing the Tartakower setup.

The early trades look like they solve Black’s problems. By move ten most of the minor pieces are gone and the queens remain. The catch is the pawn on d5, which has nowhere to go.

After …c5 and …bxc5, Black owns a pair of hanging pawns. They hold the centre, but no pawn can defend them, so every trade of pieces makes them look more like a target.

Fischer traded his knight for the bishop on e6, which removed a defender of d5, and then played 20.e4. Black’s pawn had to move forward before its pieces could support it.

The rest of the game is a slow squeeze. When it ended, Spassky is reported to have joined the audience in applauding.
